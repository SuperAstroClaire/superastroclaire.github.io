The Multiple Origins of Spurs in Stellar Streams

A challenge for dark matter diagnostics

Spurs, features that stick out from the main track of a stellar stream, are often interpreted as the signature of an encounter with a dark matter subhalo, and have been proposed as a probe of the clustering and nature of dark matter. But subhalos are not the only possible perturbers. This project asks a simple question: can other components of a galaxy produce the same features? The study is presented in our paper The multiple origins of spurs in stellar streams: a challenge for dark matter diagnostics (Guillaume et al., 2026).

Three Perturbers, One Stream

We evolved the same set of 36 stellar streams, produced by globular clusters, in four versions of a Milky-Way-like galaxy: an unperturbed axisymmetric disc (reference), a galaxy undergoing a 1:10 merger, a galaxy hosting a stellar bar, and a galaxy whose dark matter halo contains a population of subhalos. Main Results

Spurs formed in 50% of the streams in the merger case, 25% with the bar, and 28% with dark matter subhalos. The spurs produced by these three mechanisms look morphologically similar, and the repeated, diverse encounters with subhalos make it impossible to link a given spur to a single event.

Spurs should therefore not be used on their own to identify the mechanism that created them, nor to constrain the nature of dark matter, unless the other perturbers have been ruled out. A more promising path is a statistical approach across many streams, combining morphology and kinematics and compared with simulations covering a wider range of subhalo populations, bars, and merger histories.
